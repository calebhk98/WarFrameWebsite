## Overview

Jade Shadows is a narrative-focused quest that centers on the Stalker, one of Warframe's most recognizable recurring antagonists. For the first time, the quest presents Stalker not as an enemy but as a protagonist -- specifically as a former human warrior known as Sorren who has devoted himself to protecting a fellow Warframe named Jade. The quest places players in the role of an observer and then an active participant in a deeply personal crisis: Jade is dying, and Stalker cannot save her alone.

The quest spans nine stages set across multiple environments, from the deep Infested corridors of Uranus to a Corpus ship on Phobos to the player's own Orbiter. Each stage advances both the mechanical objectives and the emotional arc of the story. Jade Shadows is longer and more cinematically dense than most Warframe quests, relying heavily on in-mission voice acting and cutscenes to convey character relationships.

Upon completion, the quest awards the Jade Warframe and unlocks the Ascension game mode on Uranus, making it both a narrative milestone and a gateway to new content.

## Prerequisites

Jade Shadows requires completion of The New War. This places it late in the main story quest line and assumes significant prior context, including familiarity with the Stalker's role, the nature of Warframes, and the events of the New War itself. Players who have not completed the preceding quests will find Jade Shadows's story beats incomplete without that foundation.

There is no Mastery Rank floor specific to Jade Shadows beyond what The New War implicitly demands. The missions involved range in challenge from moderate to high, appropriate for a player who has cleared the main story.

## Stages

### The Lair

The quest opens with the Tenno navigating deep into Uranus in pursuit of a signal. Hunhow, the ancient Sentient whose consciousness resides in the blade Paracesis, provides exposition about the Stalker's identity and origin. Players learn that the Stalker was once a member of Natah's Orokin guard before the fall -- a human who chose to become a Warframe rather than die. The stage concludes with an encounter with Jade, injured and barely conscious.

### Anything But That

To obtain the materials needed to stabilize Jade, players infiltrate a Corpus ship on Phobos in a Spy mission. The objective is to crack four vaults and successfully extract data from at least three of them. The Corpus ship tileset is familiar, but the pressure of the narrative context gives the mission a distinct urgency. Bioplasma, the critical compound needed, is located in the vault data.

### Mistreatment

With the Bioplasma secured, players return to deliver the medicine. The delivery does not go smoothly: Corpus forces stage an ambush. Players must fight through the assault to keep the medicine safe. This stage combines a delivery objective with combat in a confined environment, emphasizing the precariousness of Jade's situation.

### To Catch A Tenno

In a structurally unusual stage, the Stalker infiltrates the player's own Orbiter. The Tenno's Operator -- the player themselves in narrative terms -- must be convinced to assist. This stage uses the Orbiter environment in a way that inverts the usual dynamic: the player is now the one being approached rather than the one pursuing. The interaction establishes a grudging cooperation between Stalker and the Tenno that carries through the rest of the quest.

### The Ancestor

The required medicine demands Cortichrome, a biological resource harvested from a Juggernaut Behemoth on Eris. Players travel to the Infested moon and defeat the boss. The Juggernaut Behemoth is a large, heavily armored Infested creature and one of the more challenging combat encounters in the quest. Its weak points and attack patterns are consistent with its appearances elsewhere in the game.

### The Impossible

With all materials gathered, players administer the completed medicine to Jade through a timing-based minigame. The minigame is not mechanically difficult, but it is presented with enough narrative weight to make failure feel consequential. The stage title reflects the odds Jade faces and the extraordinary circumstances surrounding her condition.

### Escape

Jade gives birth, and the Corpus resume their assault. Players carry the newborn child through a hostile corridor using the Hate scythe -- Stalker's iconic weapon, now in the Tenno's hands for this sequence. The weapon's handling and the environmental hazards create a running-and-carrying escape sequence that is among the more memorable mission structures in recent quest design.

### Past and Future

Players reach Lua, the Orokin moon that serves as a recurring threshold location in Warframe's story. Here the player makes a choice: name the child Sirius or Orion. The choice is cosmetic in its narrative impact but represents one of the few moments in the quest where the player has agency over an outcome. The location on Lua connects the child's existence to the broader Orokin legacy.

### Unfinished Business

The quest's final stage returns to the Orbiter for reward distribution and epilogue dialogue. Stalker's relationship to the Tenno is left in a state of complex tension: not friendship, but no longer pure hostility either. The Jade Warframe blueprint is awarded, along with the cosmetic items and the Ascension mode unlock.

## Rewards

Completing Jade Shadows awards the following:

- Jade Warframe blueprint -- allows construction of the Jade Warframe in the Foundry after obtaining her component blueprints separately.
- Stalker's Lair Captura scene -- a photography environment depicting the Stalker's hidden stronghold on Uranus.
- Alone, United, and Protector Portraits -- Orbiter decorations depicting imagery associated with the quest's themes.
- Ascension game mode unlock -- a new mission type on Uranus that becomes playable after quest completion.

Jade's component blueprints are not all awarded directly through the quest; some must be obtained through Ascension missions or other post-quest activities.

## Lore

The Stalker's background has been hinted at across years of Warframe content. Jade Shadows makes it explicit: he was once called Sorren, a loyal soldier of the Orokin who survived the empire's collapse by becoming a Warframe. His vendetta against the Tenno stems from witnessing the Orokin Betrayal -- the event in which the Tenno turned on and killed the Orokin masters -- a perspective that casts him as a figure of genuine grief rather than simple malevolence.

Jade herself is presented as the emotional counterweight to Sorren's anger. Her warmth and her willingness to trust stand in contrast to his isolation. That a child -- an unprecedented biological event in a setting defined by sterile Orokin engineering -- emerges from their relationship signals a thematic break in the lore: something genuinely new in a world defined by ancient cycles.

Hunhow's role as narrator is significant. The Sentient, himself a figure of immense age and complicated loyalties, provides the historical frame for Sorren's origins without apparent malice. This marks a continued evolution of Hunhow away from straightforward antagonism and toward something more ambiguous.

## Tips

- The Spy mission on Phobos requires successfully extracting three of four vaults. Bring a Warframe with strong mobility or invisibility to minimize alert risk. Loki, Ash, and Ivara are reliable options.
- The Juggernaut Behemoth on Eris has armored weak points on its underbelly. Corrosive damage performs well against its armor layer; bring consistent single-target damage for the encounter.
- The Hate scythe provided during the Escape stage handles differently from most melee weapons. Its attack animations are wide, which is useful for crowd control in the corridor environment.
- The child's name choice (Sirius or Orion) is a cosmetic narrative decision with no gameplay effect. Both names are valid options with no mechanical difference.
- Ascension missions on Uranus, unlocked after the quest, serve as the primary source for Jade's additional component blueprints.
