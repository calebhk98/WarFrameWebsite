## Overview

Chimera Prologue is a short but lore-critical quest released in Update 23.10 on October 12, 2018. It functions as a direct sequel to The Sacrifice, picking up the narrative thread left unresolved at that quest's conclusion. The WFCD data describes it simply: "Investigate your personal quarters upon completion of The Sacrifice." That description understates the quest's significance -- Chimera Prologue sets the stage for the events of The New War and introduces the Paracesis, a melee weapon engineered specifically to fight Sentient enemies.

The quest is short in playtime but dense in narrative content. It requires the Operator form throughout most of its active stages and places strong emphasis on environmental storytelling and stealth. There is no extended combat in the conventional sense; instead, Chimera Prologue relies on Void-based movement and detection avoidance to convey the threat level of the forces at work.

As a prologue-format quest, Chimera Prologue does not stand alone thematically. Players who have not completed The Sacrifice will be missing critical context for every scene. The characters, visual imagery, and dialogue are all direct continuations of that earlier quest's ending. This design choice is intentional -- Chimera Prologue is meant to be experienced immediately after The Sacrifice, functioning less as a self-contained story and more as an extended epilogue that opens a new chapter.

Completing the quest rewards the Paracesis blueprint, which is notable because Paracesis is one of the few melee weapons with a base resistance to Sentient damage adaptation -- a property that becomes relevant in endgame content and later story quests. The Ropalolyst boss node on Jupiter also unlocks upon completion, providing access to the Wisp Warframe's component blueprints.

## Prerequisites

Chimera Prologue has a single prerequisite:

- **The Sacrifice** -- This main quest must be completed in full. The Sacrifice ends with a significant narrative event, and the personal quarters disturbance that initiates Chimera Prologue occurs as a direct consequence of those events. Without completing The Sacrifice, the trigger for Chimera Prologue will not appear.

No Mastery Rank requirement is associated with this quest beyond what The Sacrifice itself demands. Players progressing through the main quest line naturally will encounter Chimera Prologue immediately after finishing The Sacrifice, provided they visit their personal quarters aboard the Orbiter.

The quest is not replayable once completed. Players who wish to review the cutscenes and story sequences can do so through the Cephalon Simaris codex after finishing.

## Stages

Chimera Prologue unfolds across four stages. The quest is brief compared to most main-line quests but compensates with narrative density in each scene.

**1. Personal Quarters**

The quest begins when the player visits the personal quarters section of the Orbiter -- the room containing the Lotus's helmet and other personal items. A familiar and unsettling presence, the Man in the Wall, manifests inside the quarters. It draws attention toward the Lotus's helmet, and when the player interacts with the helmet, they are transported without warning to a location on Lua. This stage is purely cinematic and navigational; there is no combat.

**2. The Reservoir on Lua**

Players arrive in Lua's Reservoir, a flooded chamber associated with earlier story quests. The Man in the Wall is present as a guide -- or perhaps a lure. As players move through the Reservoir in Operator form, they encounter three instances of what appear to be the Lotus, confined within energy barriers. These manifestations are not enemies in the conventional sense but represent echoes or reflections. Moving between them and progressing through the area requires careful traversal and observation.

The Reservoir section uses Lua's distinctive environmental aesthetic: white stone corridors, standing water, and dim light filtering through Orokin architecture. Enemies are present in the form of Sentient units and do not need to be engaged directly. The stage ends when the player reaches the Chamber of the Lotus at the far end of the Reservoir.

**3. The Sentient Chamber**

The quest's most mechanically distinct stage takes place inside a chamber built or modified by Sentient influence. Players must move through the space using Void Sling and Void Dash to reach marked positions while avoiding detection by a figure that patrols the environment. This figure is Ballas, the Orokin Executor last seen at the end of The Sacrifice, now visibly altered by exposure to Sentient corruption. His form has changed -- Sentient growth is visible across his body.

Detection triggers a fail state, requiring the player to restart from the most recent checkpoint. The stealth mechanic is deliberate and serves the narrative: players are witnesses to something they are not meant to see, and the asymmetry of power between the Operator and the corrupted Ballas is conveyed through gameplay rather than dialogue. Movement must be timed around Ballas's patrol route and sightlines.

This stage also provides environmental context for the Sentient threat. The chamber itself bears the architectural signatures of Sentient design philosophy -- organic curves and adaptive materials at odds with Orokin geometric precision.

**4. Return to the Orbiter**

After reaching the exit point, players return to the Orbiter. A cutscene plays involving the Man in the Wall. Upon its conclusion, the Paracesis blueprint appears in the personal quarters. The quest ends here, with the broader implications of what was witnessed left deliberately open.

## Rewards

Completing Chimera Prologue grants the following:

- **Paracesis blueprint** -- The main blueprint for the Paracesis melee weapon, a heavy blade developed to combat Sentient enemies. Paracesis is notable for its innate ability to bypass Sentient damage adaptation -- a passive property that makes it more effective against Sentient units than standard weapons that must retune their damage types after each hit. Crafting the weapon requires additional materials and a lengthy construction time. The Paracesis Sheath cosmetic decoration can be purchased from Cephalon Simaris after completing the quest.
- **Ropalolyst node access** -- The Ropalolyst boss node on Jupiter becomes available. The Ropalolyst is a large Sentient creature that serves as a weekly assassination target and drops the blueprints for Wisp Warframe's component parts (Neuroptics, Chassis, and Systems). Players seeking to acquire Wisp must defeat the Ropalolyst at least three times across separate runs to obtain all components.

The quest rewards are meaningful for mid-to-late game progression. The Paracesis is not simply a story reward; it remains a practical weapon for endgame content involving Sentient enemies, including certain segments of The New War and post-New-War activities. The Ropalolyst unlock adds a new boss encounter that players will return to repeatedly when farming Wisp.

## Lore

<Callout type="warn">The following section contains major story spoilers for Chimera Prologue and The Sacrifice.</Callout>

The Sacrifice concluded with Ballas, the Orokin Executor who had secretly maintained ties to the Sentient Natah -- later known as the Lotus -- revealing himself as a traitor to the Orokin order. He was taken by Natah at the quest's end, departing with her into the unknown. Chimera Prologue reveals the outcome of that departure: Ballas has been physically transformed, Sentient tissue growing through and over his body, remaking him as something between Orokin and Sentient.

The transformation of Ballas is not presented as a punishment but as an integration. Natah, whose identity as the Lotus was disrupted and is now being actively suppressed or overwritten, is working toward a larger purpose. The Sentient faction, led by Natah's progenitor Erra, is preparing for a large-scale campaign against the Origin System -- an event that will eventually manifest as The New War. Ballas, remade, is a component of that preparation.

The Man in the Wall's presence throughout the quest adds a layer of ambiguity. This entity, introduced in Chains of Harrow and associated with the Void, has its own inscrutable agenda. Its role in guiding -- or manipulating -- the Operator toward witnessing the scene in the Sentient chamber is never explained directly. Whether the encounter was meant as a warning, a test, or something else entirely is left unresolved.

The Paracesis itself carries narrative weight. The blueprint appears in the personal quarters after the Orbiter sequence, implying that it was placed there deliberately, by either the Man in the Wall or a force associated with it. A weapon designed to harm Sentients left as a gift at the moment the Sentient threat becomes visible is not coincidence. Chimera Prologue plants seeds that The New War and later content will cultivate.
