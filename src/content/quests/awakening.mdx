## Overview

Awakening is the mandatory introductory quest that every new player completes upon first logging into Warframe. It serves simultaneously as a tutorial and as the opening chapter of Warframe's overarching narrative. No prerequisites exist -- it triggers automatically on first login -- and it cannot be skipped. Players who have already completed it can replay it from the Codex under the Quests section.

The quest introduces the two most important figures in early-game storytelling: the Lotus, a mysterious guide who communicates through the player's Warframe, and Captain Vor, a Grineer commander obsessed with capturing and controlling the Tenno. It also introduces Cephalon Ordis, the orbiter's AI caretaker, who greets the player at the end of the tutorial and serves as a recurring presence throughout the game.

From a mechanical standpoint, Awakening teaches the foundational systems that underpin every other activity in Warframe: movement, melee combat, ranged combat, hacking terminals, and basic navigation aboard the orbiter. It concludes with the player in possession of a starter Warframe, a set of starting weapons, and a functional Liset orbiter, ready to access the star chart through Vor's Prize.

The quest is set entirely on a Grineer Galleon tileset and features no open-world traversal or complex mission types. The pacing is linear and guided, with the Lotus providing objectives and contextual narration throughout. The opening cinematic -- depicting three Warframes engaging Grineer and Dax soldiers in the distant past -- establishes the visual tone and hints at the lore surrounding the Old War without delivering direct exposition.

## Prerequisites

Awakening has no prerequisites. It launches automatically the first time a new account reaches the main menu after the game client is installed. Players who wish to replay the quest after completing it can do so through the Codex, accessible from the orbiter. Replaying the quest does not reset equipment choices or progress.

## Stages

Awakening is structured as a guided linear sequence. Each stage introduces one or two core mechanics before advancing to the next.

**1. Opening cinematic**

The quest opens with a pre-rendered cinematic showing three Warframes -- Excalibur, Mag, and Volt -- fighting alongside Dax soldiers against a Grineer invasion. The sequence ends with the Warframes entering cryosleep in a Grineer-controlled temple, establishing the time gap between the Old War and the present day. This cinematic can be viewed independently from the Quest Codex after completion.

**2. Warframe selection**

Before any gameplay begins, the player is presented with three starter Warframe choices: Excalibur, a melee-oriented frame with a strong balance of offensive and survivability tools; Mag, a caster frame specializing in crowd control and shield manipulation; and Volt, a mobile frame focused on electricity-based damage and sprint speed buffs. This decision is permanent for the starter slot but does not prevent acquisition of any frame later through normal play.

**3. Movement and combat tutorial**

The player awakens inside a Grineer Galleon and receives instruction from the Lotus on basic movement mechanics. The game teaches sprinting, crouching, and melee combat during the first encounter with Grineer Lancers. Following the movement section, the player is directed to collect a secondary weapon and is taught basic aiming and firing. The tutorial progresses through the Galleon's corridors, adding new mechanics at each gate.

**4. Weapon selection**

Mid-way through the Galleon escape, the player chooses additional starter weapons. Three categories are covered: melee, secondary, and primary. The available options at each selection point vary and offer early choices that influence the early-game arsenal. These choices, like the Warframe selection, can all be obtained again later through crafting or purchase.

**5. Hacking**

A locked door requires the player to complete a hacking minigame. This introduces the Corpus-style and Grineer-style puzzle terminals that appear throughout the game's missions. Successful hacking requires rotating or flipping tiles to complete a circuit within a time limit.

**6. Liset recovery**

After escaping the Galleon section, the player locates their dormant Liset orbiter in the ship bay. Cephalon Ordis, the orbiter's AI, assists in powering it back up. This stage introduces the concept of the orbiter as the player's home base and primary hub for mission selection, crafting, and customization.

**7. Confrontation with Captain Vor**

Before extraction completes, Captain Vor intercepts the player. Vor is a recurring antagonist in the early game; he is notable for his oratory style and his fixation on the Tenno. Rather than fighting, Vor delivers a monologue and retreats, affixing the Ascaris device to the player's Warframe. The Ascaris becomes a plot thread in the follow-on quest, Vor's Prize, which begins immediately after Awakening concludes.

Awakening ends with the player aboard their orbiter, the Ascaris device embedded in their Warframe. The Lotus advises that the device must be removed before it fully activates. This creates the hook for Vor's Prize, which tasks the player with hunting down Vor and removing the Ascaris.

## Rewards

Awakening's rewards are equipment that forms the foundation of the player's starting arsenal:

- **Starter Warframe** -- One of Excalibur, Mag, or Volt, depending on the player's choice during the quest. Each arrives fully assembled at rank 0.
- **Starter melee weapon** -- Selected during the quest from a small pool of beginner-appropriate options.
- **Starter secondary weapon** -- Selected during the quest.
- **Starter primary weapon** -- Selected during the quest.
- **Liset orbiter** -- The player's personal spacecraft, which serves as the hub for all future activity. Access to the Arsenal, Navigation, Foundry, and Codex are all located aboard the Liset.

No Platinum, Ducats, or tradable items are awarded by Awakening. The quest's rewards are exclusively starter equipment. None of the weapons or the starter frame are tradable, but each can eventually be replaced or supplemented through crafting and acquisition from other sources.

## Lore

The Warframes are constructs of the Orokin, the ancient civilization that once ruled the Origin System during a period known as the Old War. The Orokin created the Tenno -- children of the Zariman Ten Zero colony ship who were transformed by exposure to the Void -- and bound them to Warframes as weapons in the war against the Sentients. When the Old War ended and the Orokin fell, the Tenno went dormant.

The Grineer, descendants of Orokin-era clone workers, rose from subjugated laborers to become the dominant military faction in the modern Origin System. Their cloning apparatus allows for rapid expansion but degrades genetic quality over successive generations. Captain Vor represents the Grineer officer class: militaristic, hierarchical, and deeply invested in the Twin Queens' agenda to subjugate or recapture the Tenno as weapons.

The Lotus, whose identity and motives are not explained during Awakening, serves as the player's guide and mission controller throughout the early game. Her role and her connection to the Tenno are developed over dozens of hours of subsequent quests. During Awakening she communicates only in crisp tactical terms, providing navigation guidance and warnings without elaborating on her own nature or loyalties.

Cephalon Ordis, introduced briefly during the Liset recovery stage, is a fragmented AI whose erratic personality is hinted at even in these first moments. Cephalons are generally Orokin-era constructs -- digital intelligences formed from the minds of condemned individuals. Ordis is notably protective of the player's Warframe and often finishes sentences about hostile intentions by replacing the hostile thought with a more cheerful phrase, a behavioral quirk tied to his fragmented state.
