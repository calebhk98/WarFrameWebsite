## Overview

The Deadlock Protocol is a mid-to-late-game solo quest that serves as the acquisition path for the [Protea](/warframes/protea) Warframe and the Xoris Glaive weapon. The quest explores the origins of the Corpus faction through the figure of Parvos Granum, its historical founder, and reveals the consequences of his survival into the present era. It is notable for introducing the Granum Void -- a pocket dimension accessible through in-mission mechanics -- and for its final boss encounter against a Protea Specter who uses time-reversal to restore her own health.

The quest is designated solo-only and cannot be completed in a squad. This design choice is intentional: the stages involve investigation and story delivery that the developers structured around a single player's pace. Players are locked into the solo instance when selecting quest missions from the Navigation panel.

The Deadlock Protocol introduced a remastered version of the Corpus fleet tileset, including new security unit types and updated visual design. Enemy behavior in Corpus missions changed with this update, introducing the Treasurer -- a special unit who appears periodically in non-quest Corpus missions and drops Granum Crowns once the quest is completed. The Treasurer system is permanently unlocked after the quest, meaning post-completion players can access Granum Void challenges in any qualifying Corpus mission.

The Xoris Glaive received particular attention in game design: it is a melee weapon that cannot be equipped with most riven mods due to balance constraints tied to specific endgame encounters involving Granum's influence. Its role in the quest's mechanics -- charged by killing Specter Particles, then detonated to damage objective targets -- is not replicated in its post-quest use as a conventional melee weapon.

Mastery Rank 4 and completion of the Vox Solaris quest are required before The Deadlock Protocol becomes available. The Vox Solaris prerequisite ties the quest narratively to Fortuna, ensuring that players have context for the Solaris United faction and their adversarial relationship with Nef Anyo before the quest's story begins.

## Prerequisites

The following requirements must be met to access The Deadlock Protocol:

- **Mastery Rank 4** -- the minimum rank required to unlock the quest. This is typically reached after ranking up a Warframe and several weapons to rank 30.
- **Complete Vox Solaris** -- the short quest that introduces Fortuna, Eudico, and the Vox Solaris resistance cell. Completing Vox Solaris is required because several Deadlock Protocol stages are set in Fortuna and reference its characters.
- **Complete the Saturn Junction** -- the star chart requirement that gates access to Neptune, where the final boss fight takes place.

The quest is solo-only and cannot be started in a squad. Players who normally play in groups must enter the quest missions independently.

## Stages

The Deadlock Protocol has seven stages combining Interception, Defense, Capture, Spy, and a unique boss encounter.

**1. Decrypt Nef's Deadlock Memorandum**

The quest opens with intelligence about an encrypted transmission from Nef Anyo invoking the Corpus Deadlock Protocol, a legal mechanism by which a claimant to the Corpus founding lineage can assume leadership of the entire faction. The player runs a three-round Interception mission on Venus to capture and decrypt the transmission. Two towers must be held across the three rounds. Nef's message claims Parvos Granum as his ancestor, which would legitimize his bid for Corpus control.

**2. Visit Eudico in Fortuna**

Eudico and the Fortuna leadership are briefed on the threat. Fortuna's residents have direct personal stakes in whether Nef retains power, given their debt bondage under his control. This stage involves dialogue and hub navigation in Fortuna rather than a combat mission. Eudico explains the historical figure of Parvos Granum and the implications of the Deadlock Protocol being activated.

**3. Learn the Fate of Missing Solaris**

A Defense-type investigation mission on Venus. Reports of missing Solaris workers draw the player to a facility where Protean Sensors -- devices tied to Parvos's legacy technology -- are located. The player locates the sensors and defends Corpus systems from counter-intrusion. Evidence gathered here points to Solaris workers being taken rather than simply disappearing.

**4. Return to Fortuna**

A return to Fortuna to report what was found. Eudico and Vox Solaris analyze the evidence and conclude that the missing workers are being moved through some kind of dimensional interface. This stage provides the operational context for the Granum Void: a pocket dimension maintained by Granum Crowns, which serve as keys. Instructions for intercepting a Crown-carrying Treasurer are provided.

**5. Beat Nef to Granum Void**

On Phobos, a Capture mission. The Treasurer is a special Corpus unit who appears in the mission carrying a Granum Crown. The player must intercept the Treasurer, reduce his health to trigger capture, and take the Granum Crown he drops. With the Crown in hand, the player locates a Granum Tribute -- a decorative Corpus installation found in the tileset -- and activates it to enter the Granum Void for the first time. The pocket dimension is encountered briefly here to establish its mechanics before the full boss encounter.

**6. Acquire Parts to Build the Xoris**

A Spy mission on Europa. Three vaults contain the Xoris component blueprints. The Xoris is a Glaive-type weapon that uses charged throw attacks and detonations. The blueprints must be built in the Foundry before proceeding to the final stage.

**7. Free Solaris Captives in Granum Void**

The final stage is a boss encounter on Neptune. The player enters the Granum Void using a Granum Crown obtained from a Treasurer in the mission. Inside, Specter Particles -- enemies unique to the Void -- must be killed with the Xoris to charge the weapon. Once charged, the Xoris is detonated near a captive pod to free trapped Solaris workers. The Protea Specter boss appears and fights using Protea's ability kit, including Temporal Anchor -- an ability that rewinds Protea's health to a previous state if she takes lethal damage. The sequence must be repeated three times with successful detonations before the encounter concludes.

<Callout type="warn">Spoilers for Parvos Granum's survival and Protea's origin follow.</Callout>

The Granum Void reveals that Parvos Granum did not die as Corpus history records. He was trapped in a temporal loop maintained by Protea's Temporal Anchor ability, which she activated continuously to prevent his death -- at the cost of remaining in an endless cycle herself. The Solaris workers being sacrificed were required as fuel for the interdimensional pocket maintaining the loop. The quest's resolution frees the workers and disrupts the loop, but Parvos himself escapes, setting up his continued involvement in post-quest content.

## Rewards

Completing The Deadlock Protocol grants:

- **Protea Warframe blueprint** -- the main blueprint for Protea. Her three component blueprints (Neuroptics, Chassis, Systems) drop from Granum Void completion in Neptune, Phobos, and Venus missions respectively, accessed post-quest through the Treasurer mechanic.
- **Xoris Glaive weapon blueprint** -- a thrown-and-detonated melee weapon with a unique explosion mechanic. The Xoris cannot benefit from Riven mods in specific endgame encounters tied to Granum's influence.
- **Granum Void Captura Scene** -- a cosmetic diorama scene for the Captura photography mode.
- **Granum Crown mechanics unlocked** -- after quest completion, Treasurers begin appearing in non-quest Corpus missions, and Granum Crowns can be collected to access Granum Void challenges for resources and Protea component farming.

If Protea or the Xoris are sold after the quest, replacement blueprints are available from Cephalon Simaris's Offerings.

## Lore

The Deadlock Protocol reframes the Corpus as a faction with genuine ideological origins rather than pure profit motive. Parvos Granum founded the Corpus on principles of meritocratic trade and accumulation, which the present Corpus has distorted into exploitation and debt bondage. Nef Anyo's invocation of the Deadlock Protocol is a cynical use of legitimate succession law, not an act of devotion to the founding principles.

Protea's design as a time-bending Warframe is inseparable from the events of the quest. Her passive ability involves grenade deployment, and her ultimate creates a Temporal Anchor that rewinds her state -- a direct reflection of how she was used by Parvos. The quest's boss fight uses Temporal Anchor against the player, requiring them to understand the mechanic before they can counter it.

## Tips

- The Protea Specter's Temporal Anchor activates when she drops below a health threshold. Damage dealt before the activation is undone when she rewinds. Focusing on charging and detonating the Xoris near her to trigger the objective sequence progresses the fight more reliably than attempting to outdamage the heal.
- Completing the Xoris detonation sequence three times ends the boss fight regardless of remaining enemy count. Prioritizing the Specter Particle kills to charge the Xoris, then positioning for detonation, is faster than sustained DPS attempts.
- The Treasurer in Stage 5 will attempt to flee the mission area if not intercepted quickly. Using mobility-focused Warframes or abilities that restrict enemy movement (Mag's Pull, Vauban's Bastille) helps secure the Crown before the Treasurer can escape.
- Granum Void enemies drop Ammo and Energy pickups after the quest, making energy management easier than during the quest encounter itself. Builds reliant on ability-heavy Warframes are more viable post-quest than during the first run.
- After completing the quest, farming all three Protea components requires running Granum Void challenges in three different missions: Neptune (Neuroptics), Phobos (Chassis), and Venus (Systems). Checking which node hosts a Treasurer spawn is useful before committing to a farming session.
