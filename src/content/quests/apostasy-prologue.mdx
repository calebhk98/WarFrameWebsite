## Overview

Apostasy Prologue is a short main story quest introduced in Update 22.8 (December 2017) that serves as a direct bridge between Chains of Harrow and The Sacrifice. It is one of the briefest quests in Warframe by design -- its purpose is atmospheric and narrative rather than mechanical -- but its story consequences are among the most significant in the entire game. The departure of the Lotus fundamentally alters the Tenno's relationship with their primary mission contact and sets the stage for the events of The Sacrifice.

The quest is initiated by interacting with a glowing violet orb that appears in the Orbiter's Personal Quarters after the Chains of Harrow quest is completed. Because the quest involves intimate personal-quarters interaction and Operator-mode sequences, it must be started solo. It is replayable through the Codex after completion, allowing players to revisit its cutscenes.

The title draws on the word "apostasy" -- the formal abandonment of a held belief or religious commitment -- and frames the Lotus's departure as a kind of desertion from the role she has occupied throughout the Tenno's entire history. The quest is brief but quietly devastating in its implications, and its three stages are unified by tone rather than combat challenge.

## Prerequisites

Apostasy Prologue requires the following before it can be initiated:

- **Chains of Harrow** -- The direct prerequisite quest. Chains of Harrow must be completed fully; the Apostasy Prologue trigger does not appear until that quest is resolved.
- **Personal Quarters Segment** -- The Orbiter's Personal Quarters module must be installed. The segment is obtained during The War Within and unlocks the customizable area of the ship where the initiating interaction takes place.
- **Solo mode** -- The quest triggers from within the player's Orbiter and must be started while not in a squad. Players already in a party should return to solo before investigating the Personal Quarters.

No Mastery Rank requirement is documented by WFCD for this quest. The challenge level is minimal; the quest contains no standard enemy combat beyond brief Operator-mode sequences.

## Stages

The quest unfolds across three stages, each brief and focused almost entirely on narrative delivery.

**1. The Orbiter's Personal Quarters**

After completing Chains of Harrow, a small glowing violet orb appears near the port window of the Orbiter's Personal Quarters. Interacting with it initiates the quest. There is no combat in this stage; its function is purely to anchor the player and provide the transition that follows. The orb acts as a remnant of Void energy responding to whatever has already begun to shift in the relationship between the Lotus and Ballas.

**2. Lua -- The Reservoir**

The quest transitions to a solo mission on Lua, the Moon, where players follow a purple luminescent trail through an Orokin-architecture corridor sequence. No enemies are present. The environment is quiet, and the trail winds toward a broken Reservoir -- one of the ancient Orokin devices connected to the Warframe program.

Along the way, players hear audio fragments of conversations between Margulis and Ballas, drawn from a time before her execution. These fragments establish the emotional and historical context for what follows: Margulis was an Archimedean scientist who loved the Void-touched children and was put to death by the Orokin for refusing to destroy them. Ballas, an Orokin Executor, mourned her. The weight of their history saturates the stage.

At the Reservoir, players descend into a pit and are transitioned into Operator form, ending the Lua portion and beginning the Void sequence.

**3. The Void -- The Jade Light**

The final stage takes place in a Void environment where players witness echoes of Margulis's final moments -- her execution by the Jade Light, the Orokin method of capital punishment. The player then encounters a pod containing the Lotus, her neural connections active, suspended in the kind of Void-adjacent space the Tenno have entered before.

Ballas appears and addresses the Lotus, calling her Margulis -- acknowledging that the Lotus is Margulis, altered and reborn through Orokin processes into a controller for the Warframe program. He apologizes for what was done to her and promises he will not leave her behind again. He disconnects her from the pod, removes her helmet, and the two figures walk together into a blinding white light.

The Operator is left behind, alone, holding the Lotus's discarded helmet. Ordis observes that the Lotus has left, and begins attempting to synthesize her mission directives independently.

## Rewards

Apostasy Prologue offers no combat rewards, relic drops, or crafting components. Its rewards are entirely narrative and cosmetic:

- **Lotus's helmet on a plinth** -- The Lotus's golden helmet is placed in the Orbiter's Personal Quarters as a permanent reminder of her departure. Players can interact with it as a keepsake.
- **Ordis transmission takeover** -- All future mission directive transmissions now display as a glitching purple Lotus-shaped hologram rather than the original interface. This reflects Ordis attempting to fill the Lotus's role while experiencing processing errors from the transition.
- **The Sacrifice unlocked** -- Completion of Apostasy Prologue is one of the required prerequisites for accessing The Sacrifice, the direct narrative continuation that follows.

The absence of material rewards is intentional. The quest's conclusion asks players to sit with the loss rather than move immediately toward a new mechanical objective.

## Lore

The Apostasy Prologue is built on revelations about Margulis that the game has been seeding since The Second Dream. Margulis was an Orokin Archimedean -- a scientist of the highest rank -- who worked with the Void-touched children aboard the Zariman Ten Zero after they were recovered by the Orokin. While other Archimedeans sought to destroy the children or weaponize them without regard for their wellbeing, Margulis chose to protect and heal them.

The Orokin sentenced her to death by the Jade Light for this act of compassion and defiance. Ballas, an Orokin Executor and one of the ruling class, had a relationship with Margulis that the game only partially discloses at this stage. His grief over her death -- and his decision to act on it -- becomes the driving force behind his manipulation of the Tenno program and, ultimately, behind the Lotus's existence.

The revelation that the Lotus is Margulis reframes the entire Warframe narrative in retrospect. The Lotus's devotion to guiding and protecting the Tenno is not merely programmed loyalty: it is the continuation of Margulis's original choice to shield the children she cared for. Her departure with Ballas is framed as a reunion long denied, though the game leaves deliberately ambiguous whether her choice to leave is fully her own or partly Ballas's manipulation.

Following the quest, Ordis's attempts to replicate the Lotus's directives produce a corrupted purple hologram, a visual reminder that the system the Tenno relied on has fractured. The Lotus's helmet in the Personal Quarters serves as a physical anchor for this absence: a reminder of what was lost and why it mattered.

## Tips

Apostasy Prologue is not mechanically demanding, but the following notes help players approach it without confusion.

- **Install the Personal Quarters Segment first.** The quest trigger does not appear if the segment is missing. The segment is obtained automatically during The War Within, but players who skipped that quest's optional unlocks may need to acquire it separately through the Clan Dojo research or market.
- **Play in solo mode.** The Orbiter interaction that starts the quest does not function in a party. Return to solo before checking the Personal Quarters.
- **Do not rush through the audio fragments.** The conversations between Margulis and Ballas heard on Lua are load-bearing for understanding the Void stage. Pausing to listen fully rewards the player with context that enhances the final cutscene.
- **Replay is available.** If the ending cutscene is missed or interrupted, the quest can be replayed from the Codex under completed quests.
