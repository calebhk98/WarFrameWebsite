## Overview

Octavia's Anthem is a story quest that rewards the Octavia Warframe Blueprint upon completion. Introduced in Update 20.0 (March 2017), the quest follows Cephalon Suda -- a synthetic intelligence devoted to the collection and preservation of knowledge -- as she investigates an ancient Orokin instrument called the Mandachord. Her obsession with the device draws the attention of Hunhow, the Sentient destroyer encountered in [Natah](/quests/natah) and [The Second Dream](/quests/the-second-dream), who uses the instrument as a vector to corrupt her datascape.

The quest is solo-only and leans heavily on platforming sequences and environmental puzzle mechanics. Players must navigate diverse tilesets -- Corpus-controlled Neptune, Grineer Sealab trenches on Uranus, the mystical Orokin Moon on Lua, and the Void itself -- to collect fragments of an ancient song. The climactic section takes place inside a virtual space constructed by Cephalon Suda's corrupted memory, requiring dexterous movement and combat against Sentient manifestations.

Octavia herself is a Warframe built entirely around music. Her abilities generate rhythmic effects synchronized to an in-game sequencer, and Octavia's Anthem introduces players to her origins while serving as an extended demonstration of the game's capacity for elaborate, theatrical storytelling.

## Prerequisites

Players must meet the following conditions before beginning Octavia's Anthem:

- **Complete The Second Dream** -- The quest is gated behind the events of [The Second Dream](/quests/the-second-dream), which introduced Operator gameplay and significantly expanded the Warframe narrative. Hunhow's role in Octavia's Anthem draws directly on his characterization in that quest.
- **Mastery Rank 3** -- A basic threshold that most players will exceed naturally before reaching The Second Dream.
- **Access to Neptune, Uranus, Lua, and Void nodes** -- The quest stages span these locations. Players progressing through The Second Dream will have unlocked most or all of these regions. Lua becomes accessible after The Second Dream's completion.

The quest key is obtained through the Codex and does not require crafting. It is a standard narrative quest accessible immediately once prerequisites are met. Simaris's relay section requires an accessible Tenno Relay on any planet.

## Stages

Octavia's Anthem unfolds across seven stages, each introducing a new environment and a new element of the ancient song.

**1. Collect Mandachord components**

Cephalon Suda contacts the Tenno, describing unusual energy readings on Neptune. A Corpus Outpost mission on Sao requires players to follow a spectral light through the facility to locate three Mandachord components scattered across the map. Standard Corpus enemies patrol the outpost. The light guide serves as both navigation aid and atmospheric device, hinting that the Mandachord has a kind of presence of its own.

**2. Build the Mandachord**

The three collected components are assembled into the Mandachord through the Foundry. This is a brief interlude between mission stages, requiring only the components recovered from Neptune and standard crafting time. The Mandachord itself is not a usable item -- it is a quest object whose assembly triggers the next phase of the investigation.

**3. Find the first song fragment**

Suda's analysis of the Mandachord reveals that it was designed to play a specific song, fragments of which are encoded in Orokin-era structures across the system. The first fragment is located in a Grineer Sealab on Uranus. Players must infiltrate the facility and locate a Hydraulus -- an ancient Orokin keyboard instrument -- and solve a musical puzzle by activating the correct sequence of notes. Grineer forces occupy the lab, requiring stealth or combat as the player prefers.

**4. Find the second song fragment**

The second fragment is embedded deeper in Orokin history: inside a facility on Lua, the Orokin Moon. Lua was hidden inside the Void at the end of the Old War and only became accessible after The Second Dream's events. Its tileset mixes Orokin golden architecture with creeping Sentient presence, and the enemies encountered here include Sentient units in addition to Corpus forces who have been attempting to excavate the moon's secrets. A second Hydraulus puzzle awaits at the fragment's location, more elaborate than the first.

**5. Amplify the signal**

With both fragments recovered, Suda determines that the song must be transmitted through a Void tower to reach its intended destination. Players breach a Void node and activate a series of transmission consoles while defending against Sentient attacks. The Sentients recognize the song and respond with hostility, confirming that the Mandachord is connected to events that predate the current era. The Operator's Void abilities are particularly effective against Sentient units encountered here, as Sentients adapt to standard damage but remain vulnerable to Void energy.

**6. Consult Cephalon Simaris**

The transmission reaches Cephalon Suda, but rather than helping her, it destabilizes her. She begins to fragment, her datascape corrupting under Hunhow's influence. Simaris, whose relationship with Suda is established through their shared nature as Cephalon entities devoted to preservation of knowledge, knows a way to reach her directly. Players visit a Tenno Relay to speak with Simaris, who provides a data-key allowing entry into Suda's compromised datascape.

**7. Final confrontation**

<Callout type="warn">This stage contains major story spoilers for Octavia's Anthem, including the nature of Hunhow's corruption and the resolution of Cephalon Suda's arc.</Callout>

The final stage places players inside a surreal arena constructed from Suda's fragmented data. The environment is a circular platform decorated with Mandachord-style musical notation, and the objective requires activating musical notes positioned across the arena while avoiding hazards that Hunhow's corruption has introduced. The encounter escalates across three phases, each increasing the complexity of the note patterns and the danger of the manifestations attacking the player.

Hunhow's presence in Suda's datascape is not his physical form -- it is a projection, an attempt to consume her accumulated knowledge and use her connection to the Tenno as a strategic asset. Defeating the manifestation breaks his hold on Suda, though she retains awareness of the intrusion. The resolution is quieter than a conventional boss fight: Suda is restored not by destruction alone but by completing the song the Mandachord was always meant to play.

## Rewards

Completing Octavia's Anthem grants:

- **Octavia Blueprint** -- The primary reward and the reason most players undertake the quest. This Blueprint is taken to the Foundry to build the Octavia Warframe, which also requires the three Octavia component Blueprints (Neuroptics, Chassis, Systems), obtained from the Lua music puzzles found in the open Lua tileset after the quest.
- **Pistol Riven Mod** -- Players who purchased Octavia directly from the Market before completing the quest receive a Pistol Riven Mod as a substitute reward, since the Warframe Blueprint would be redundant.
- **Narrative progression** -- The quest deepens the Cephalon lore and adds further context to Hunhow's capabilities and goals following The Second Dream. It also establishes the Mandachord as a persistent in-game system that Octavia players use to compose music for their abilities.

## Lore

The Mandachord is an Orokin instrument of remarkable sophistication, designed to encode and transmit information through music. In the Orokin era, the Cephalon entities served as archivists and navigational systems, and some were connected to musical traditions as a form of data encoding. Cephalon Suda's particular devotion to knowledge preservation drew her to investigate the Mandachord when anomalous energy readings suggested it had been activated somewhere in the Origin System.

Hunhow's ability to exploit Suda's obsession reflects his understanding of the Orokin systems that created both the Cephalon entities and the Tenno. He recognizes the Mandachord as a frequency that resonates with Cephalon architecture, and he uses Suda's investigation as an entry point. His goal is not to destroy Suda -- it is to consume her, to absorb her accumulated knowledge base and her relay connections as intelligence assets against the Tenno.

The quest's resolution does not eliminate Hunhow. His influence in the Origin System persists well beyond Octavia's Anthem, threading through subsequent quests. However, Suda survives and continues her work. Octavia herself, as a Warframe built around music as a weapon and a tool, carries some echo of the Mandachord's original purpose into combat.

## Tips

- The final platforming arena in Stage 7 requires precise movement. Warframes with strong mobility -- those with double-jump augments or innate flight capabilities -- make the note-activation sequences significantly easier.
- Operator Void Blast is effective against Sentient enemies in Stages 4 and 7. Sentients adapt to conventional damage types, making Void energy a reliable fallback when weapons lose effectiveness.
- The Hydraulus puzzles in Stages 3 and 4 use audio cues as well as visual indicators. If the Effects Intensity accessibility setting is low, some visual effects may be hard to see. Enabling that setting helps with note identification.
- Octavia's component blueprints for completing her build after the quest drop from Rotation C of the Lua Crossfire missions and from specific Lua Spy vaults. The quest itself provides only the main Blueprint.
- The quest is solo-only; no squad matchmaking is available. Ensure the Warframe and loadout chosen are self-sufficient before starting.
