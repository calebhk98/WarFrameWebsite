## Overview

Oro is a core resource and scoring mechanic tied to the lore and gameplay of Conclave. In narrative terms, Oro is described as the life-force or spiritual essence of a Warframe -- a fragment of the Orokin transference bond that ties the Operator to their frame. In gameplay terms, Oro functions as a score token in certain Conclave modes, dropped by a slain player and collectible by any participant.

The Oro concept bridges the mechanical and the lore layers of Conclave. Teshin's framing of PvP combat as genuine life-threatening training is grounded in the idea that each Warframe carries Oro -- that there is real spiritual cost to each defeat, even when the physical frame is rebuilt on respawn. Collecting an opponent's Oro acknowledges the gravity of the exchange in a way that a simple kill counter does not.

Oro does not refer to a specific weapon or item in inventory. It is the name for the dropped token in modes where it functions as a scoring object, and it also appears in the broader lore surrounding the Stalker and the origins of Warframe sacrifice.

## Rules and mechanics

In Oro-collection variants of Annihilation, each kill drops a glowing Oro token at the death location. Players must physically move to and collect the token to add the kill to their score. This creates a significant mechanic difference from standard kill-count Annihilation: the kill itself is not the score event -- the collection is.

Uncollected Oro tokens persist for a short window before dissolving. Another player -- including the player who made the kill -- must reach the token in time. This punishes passive sniping from across the map, because the sniper cannot safely collect their own tokens without closing distance and exposing themselves to counterplay.

Opponents can contest a token drop location. A player killed near the token they were attempting to collect effectively scores a point for whoever finishes them and reaches the token first. The mechanic introduces aggressive recovery play, where defending a fresh token location with immediate counterattack is more efficient than ceding the area.

## Tactics

Token contention creates predictable engagements at recent death sites. Players who understand this cycle rotate to known kill zones immediately after scoring, expecting opposition to arrive. This also applies in reverse: if an opponent scored a kill nearby, suppressing their collection attempt while a teammate collects the token is a strong two-player tactic.

Fast-moving frames with strong sprint and slide stats under Conclave scaling benefit disproportionately in Oro-collection modes because physical distance to tokens is the gating factor for score accumulation. A player who kills but cannot reach the token before an opponent does generates nothing.

Zone control rather than pure kill-chasing is often the more efficient approach. Holding a central area of the arena increases the probability of being physically close to any token that drops nearby, regardless of who made the kill. This is a counter-intuitive shift from kill-focused play but is common in experienced Conclave groups.

## Weapon balance

Weapon selection in Oro-collection modes follows the same Conclave-balanced ruleset as other modes. Because reaching tokens quickly after kills is essential, weapons that facilitate rapid engagement and disengagement -- automatic rifles, shotguns at close range, and mobile melee -- are more consistent than sniper-class options that leave the player stranded at range after scoring.

High fire-rate weapons that can confirm kills quickly and allow immediate movement toward the token outperform precision platforms in the Oro-collection context, assuming the player has adequate aim to close engagements efficiently.

## Rewards

Oro-collection Annihilation variants award Conclave standing on the same formula as other match types. The mode's score mechanic makes it no more or less rewarding than standard Annihilation from a standing perspective. Players choose the mode based on the gameplay style it produces rather than any differential reward.

## Lore

Oro in lore predates Conclave's existence as a game mode. The concept appears in early Warframe lore fragments as a description of the spiritual cost of Orokin transference -- the link that allows an Operator to inhabit and animate a Warframe. Killing a Warframe in the Orokin era was understood to involve severing this Oro link, a violent metaphysical act beyond the physical destruction of the frame itself.

Teshin incorporated the Oro mechanic into Conclave to make explicit what he believes should be implicit in all Tenno combat: that every defeat carries meaning beyond the tactical. Collecting an opponent's Oro is, in his framing, an acknowledgment that the fight was real and had stakes, even within a training context. The token is both score and symbol.
