## Overview

Capture the Cephalon is Conclave's objective-based team mode, directly analogous to capture-the-flag formats in other games. Each team defends a Cephalon fragment at their base while simultaneously attempting to steal the opposing team's fragment and return it to their own base for a capture point. The match is won by the team that accumulates the required number of captures before time expires.

The mode emphasizes role differentiation. A team needs at least one player consistently attempting captures, one defending the home Cephalon from theft attempts, and ideally one or two supporting both objectives. The flat three or four players per side in standard matches makes this role distribution a constant negotiation based on the flow of each round.

Conclave's standard balanced ruleset applies: all Warframes operate on flat health and armor profiles, weapons deal Conclave-adjusted damage, and abilities are restricted or capped to prevent single-ability dominance. This keeps Cephalon captures dependent on map awareness and movement skill rather than ability burst.

## Rules and mechanics

The Cephalon fragment is a physical object present at each team's base. Picking it up puts the carrier in possession mode. The carrier can still move, attack, and use abilities, but cannot restore health from pickups while holding the fragment. If the carrier is killed, the fragment drops at that location and remains there for a short window before returning to base automatically if no player picks it up.

Capturing the opposing team's Cephalon requires returning it to the home base while the home Cephalon is also present there. If the home Cephalon has been stolen, captures are blocked until it is recovered. This rule creates the familiar split-decision scenario: should a player chase down the stolen home fragment or continue pushing the enemy fragment?

Match length defaults to a capture limit, commonly three captures, or a time limit -- whichever arrives first. At time expiration, the team with more captures wins. Ties go to the team with more kills, giving combat value even in losing capture attempts.

## Tactics

The carrier's movement is the single most important factor in a capture attempt. Bullet-jump and slide mechanics preserve full speed while carrying the fragment. Routes that minimize exposure to the defending team's sightlines extend the carrier's survival window. Learning the shortest route from the enemy base to the home base is the first investment for any consistent capture player.

Escort play -- where one or two teammates clear the path ahead of the carrier -- dramatically increases capture success rates. The defenders must split between targeting the carrier and the escorts, and a well-timed distraction from an escort can open a clear run to the base.

On defense, positioning near but not on the Cephalon fragment prevents opponents from walking directly to it uncontested. Proximity mines, area-denial movement, and sightline control from adjacent corridors are more effective than a single player sitting on top of the fragment, which creates a predictable kill location that opponents can bait out.

## Weapon balance

Conclave weapon balance in Capture the Cephalon shares the mode-wide ruleset. However, weapon selection in this mode skews toward versatility over specialization because players frequently alternate between offensive capture roles and defensive positions within a single match.

Shotguns and close-range automatics suit the tight corridors of most Capture the Cephalon arenas. Sniper-class weapons are viable for covering open base approaches but leave the player exposed when the situation requires switching to close defense. Most experienced Capture the Cephalon players carry a general-purpose primary and a close-range secondary.

## Rewards

Capture the Cephalon awards Conclave standing under the same formula as other Conclave modes. Captures contribute a bonus multiplier on top of the baseline participation standing. Winning the match provides an additional win bonus. The standing earned accumulates toward Teshin Conclave Syndicate ranks, unlocking augment mods, cosmetics, and sigils.

Players who focus this mode to grind standing efficiently find that consistent capture attempts -- even failed ones -- accumulate standing faster than purely defensive or kill-focused play in a single match.

## Lore

Teshin chose the Cephalon fragment as the capture object deliberately. In Orokin-era doctrine, Cephalons were strategic assets -- computational minds that coordinated fleet logistics, orbital defense grids, and intelligence operations. Capturing and denying enemy Cephalon access was a high-priority wartime objective.

By centering the mode on a Cephalon fragment, Teshin roots the exercise in a historically grounded scenario. The Tenno who compete in Capture the Cephalon are, in his framing, rehearsing the extraction and denial tactics that protected or compromised critical infrastructure during the Old War.
