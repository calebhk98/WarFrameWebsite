## Overview

Annihilation is the foundational free-for-all mode of Conclave, the Player vs. Player combat system in Warframe. Every participant competes independently, with no allies and no coordinated objectives beyond eliminating opponents. The mode strips away cooperative mechanics and forces direct engagement with other Warframes at close to medium range across Conclave-specific arenas.

Matches use Conclave-balanced loadouts, which override the standard PvE stats of all Warframes and weapons. Every frame operates on a flat health and armor profile defined by the Conclave ruleset, and all weapons deal Conclave-specific damage values. This balancing layer is necessary because raw PvE stats vary wildly and would produce unplayable lopsided matches.

Annihilation is typically the entry point for players exploring Conclave for the first time. It requires no team coordination, which removes the communication overhead that modes like Capture the Cephalon demand. New PvP participants can focus on learning Conclave movement mechanics, weapon feel under the balanced ruleset, and the distinct map geometry of each arena.

## Rules and mechanics

The match ends when the first player reaches the score limit, which defaults to 25 kills, or when the round timer expires. The player with the highest kill count at time expiration wins; ties are broken by fewest deaths.

Respawning is automatic and rapid, placing the player at a random spawn point away from active combat. Kill streaks are tracked but do not grant power bonuses as they do in standard PvE modes -- Conclave deliberately avoids snowball mechanics that reward the already-winning player.

Friendly fire does not apply because the mode has no teams. All hits deal damage to the target. Status effects from procs apply under Conclave-specific durations, which are generally shorter than their PvE equivalents to prevent crowd-control dominance.

Energy pickups are present in the arenas. Ability costs are unchanged from their Conclave-adjusted values. Most warframe abilities are restricted or have their damage capped within Conclave, so mobility and positioning carry greater weight than raw ability power.

## Tactics

Controlling the center of a map denies the majority of sightlines to opponents and provides faster access to energy and health orbs. This advantage comes with the risk of being targeted from multiple directions simultaneously.

Slide attacks deal Conclave-adjusted melee damage and are faster to initiate than standing attacks, making them a common pressure tool at close range. Bullet-jump movement allows rapid repositioning and doubles as an evasion technique because it temporarily increases the player's hitbox displacement speed.

Warframes with higher base sprint speed under Conclave scaling, such as Loki, reach engagements and escape range more quickly, though the flat health normalization reduces the defensive advantage that heavy frames carry in PvE.

Weapon selection matters significantly. Shotguns excel at short ranges inside the tighter corridors of arenas like Ose on Europa, while rifles favor the longer sightlines on maps like Station on Ceres. Secondary weapons provide a fallback when the primary runs out of ammo, and managing ammo economy is a recurring skill challenge in extended Annihilation matches.

## Weapon balance

Conclave applies independent damage multipliers and projectile behavior changes to every weapon in the mode. Weapons that dominate PvE -- for instance, high-multishot launchers or explosive-area weapons -- have their Conclave damage and blast radius reduced to prevent single-weapon dominance.

Hitscan weapons function identically to PvE regarding projectile travel, but projectile-based weapons require leading targets in motion. The Braton and Dera families are often cited as baseline options for new Conclave players because their Conclave values fall close to the mean across all ranges.

Melee combat rewards timing over spam. Blocking interrupts some incoming damage, and timing a counter-attack after a blocked strike opens a brief window of free damage. Stances activate their normal combo sequences but at reduced damage multipliers compared to PvE.

## Rewards

Completing Annihilation matches generates Conclave standing, which counts toward ranks in the Teshin Conclave Syndicate. Standing is awarded per match regardless of finish position, with a bonus multiplier for placing in the top half. Win bonuses provide additional standing for first-place finishes.

Standing is used at the Conclave Syndicate to purchase Conclave-exclusive augment mods, Warframe skins, sigils, and weapon skins. Several augments obtained through Conclave apply to PvE gameplay and are considered desirable by the broader community, making Annihilation a viable path toward them even for players with no sustained PvP interest.

Daily standing caps apply as with all syndicates. The cap scales with the player's current Conclave rank. Consistent match participation across multiple sessions reaches the cap faster than grinding single long matches.

## Lore

Conclave derives its name and framing from Teshin Dax, the Conclave Master. Teshin believes that combat between Tenno is necessary preparation for the trials that lie ahead. He does not endorse Annihilation as a sport; in his framing, every match is a training exercise, a controlled violence that keeps the Tenno sharp against the day when complacency could mean extinction.

The arenas themselves are repurposed locations across the Origin System. Station on Ceres uses a decommissioned Grineer facility. Teshin selects environments where real engagements occurred during the Old War or during the Grineer-Corpus conflicts, lending historical weight to what might otherwise be pure competition.
