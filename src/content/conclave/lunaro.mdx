## Overview

Lunaro is a sport mode within Conclave that functions as a non-lethal ball game. Two teams compete to throw the Lunaro ball into the opposing team's goal. Players cannot deal damage or kill opponents directly in Lunaro -- the entire mode is built around ball possession, passing, blocking, and scoring. It is the most mechanically distinct mode in Conclave and the only one in which traditional combat skill is secondary to game-sense and positioning.

Lunaro was added to Warframe as a dedicated sport mode, drawing loose thematic inspiration from ancient Orokin athletic traditions. It serves as an alternative Conclave entry point for players who want to earn Conclave standing without engaging in direct PvP combat. Teshin treats Lunaro as a legitimate training discipline, arguing that spatial awareness, teamwork, and controlled movement under pressure are attributes shared by both athletes and warriors.

The mode is played in a symmetrical stadium arena. Both goals are positioned at opposite ends, and the ball starts at the center. Movement, passing, and shot mechanics all use the existing Warframe parkour system, which gives the mode a high mechanical ceiling even though lethal combat is absent.

## Rules and mechanics

Players pick up the Lunaro ball by moving over it. The carrier can pass to a teammate using a dedicated throw action or attempt to score by throwing the ball into the opposing goal. The carrier can sprint and bullet-jump while holding the ball, which is how high-speed plays and breakaway scoring attempts are executed.

Opponents can knock the ball loose from a carrier by using a physical contact action -- a melee-based bump that displaces the carrier and drops the ball. This contact is the closest thing to combat in Lunaro. It does not deal health damage but can interrupt a carry or disrupt a shot attempt if timed correctly.

Goaltenders -- a role any player can occupy -- can block incoming shots by positioning in front of the goal and triggering a block action. A successful block deflects the ball back into the field. The block window is finite, so anticipating shot timing is required for consistent goaltending.

Matches last a set time with the higher-scoring team winning at expiration. Ties result in sudden-death overtime, where the next goal scored determines the outcome.

## Tactics

Passing chains between teammates advance the ball faster than solo carries and are harder for opponents to intercept, because a pass in motion requires predicting where the receiver will be. A well-executed pass-and-run play -- where the carrier passes ahead to a teammate already in motion toward goal -- frequently bypasses the entire defending team.

Positioning one player near the opposing goal at kickoff, anticipating a quick pass from the center pickup, creates early scoring pressure. This is commonly called a long-pass start and is among the highest-percentage early-game tactics in Lunaro.

Goaltending skill centers on reading the carrier's body orientation during a shot attempt. Experienced Lunaro players learn the ball trajectory from different carry positions and angle their blocks preemptively rather than reacting to the throw animation.

## Weapon balance

Weapon balance is not applicable in Lunaro. All offensive Warframe abilities and weapons are disabled during matches. The only physical interaction between players is the contact bump, which deals no damage. This makes Lunaro the one Conclave mode where loadout selection has no impact on match outcome.

Frame selection matters marginally -- sprint speed and bullet-jump strength under Conclave scaling vary slightly between frames, and faster-moving frames can reach the center ball and execute breakaway passes more quickly. The practical difference is small enough that team cohesion and game-sense outweigh frame choice for most skill levels.

## Rewards

Lunaro awards Conclave standing per match using the same participation-plus-win-bonus formula as combat modes. Standing accumulates toward Teshin Conclave Syndicate ranks. The same rewards -- augment mods, cosmetics, sigils, and skins -- are unlocked regardless of which Conclave mode generates the standing.

Lunaro is sometimes the preferred standing-grind path for players who find direct PvP combat in Annihilation or Team Annihilation frustrating but still want access to Conclave-exclusive PvE augments.

## Lore

Lunaro is described by Teshin as an ancient sport originating in pre-Orokin cultures, later adopted by Orokin-era warrior castes as a training complement to armed combat. The sport enforced precision movement, spatial reasoning, and cooperative timing without the psychological weight of actual killing, which made it palatable as a peacetime athletic tradition and as a structured training exercise within the Dax warrior academies.

The name "Lunaro" has no confirmed in-game etymological breakdown, but community analysis links it to celestial lunar imagery, consistent with the Orokin habit of naming cultural artifacts with astronomical references. Teshin reintroduced Lunaro to the Conclave roster alongside combat modes as part of his argument that the Tenno need more than killing instinct -- they need the discipline of competitive play under structured rules.
