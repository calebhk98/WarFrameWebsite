## Overview

Team Annihilation is Conclave's team deathmatch mode. Two squads of players compete to accumulate kills against the opposing team and reach the score limit before the timer expires. Unlike Annihilation, where every player is a target, Team Annihilation introduces a cooperative layer: protecting teammates extends their availability as combatants and denies the opposing team easy score increments.

The mode shares the core Conclave ruleset: all Warframes operate on balanced health, armor, and energy profiles, and all weapons deal Conclave-specific damage values. Team composition matters in a different way here than in PvE -- survivability under focus fire is higher, and frames with disruption abilities can pull opponents off a teammate under attack.

Team Annihilation is considered intermediate in Conclave's mode difficulty. New participants benefit from having allies who can revive them and provide overlapping fire, reducing the immediate pressure of a full free-for-all environment.

## Rules and mechanics

Matches run until one team reaches the score limit, typically 50 combined kills, or until the round timer expires. At expiration, the team with the higher kill count wins. Team kills do not count toward the score, and friendly fire is disabled to prevent deliberate griefing.

Revive mechanics differ from standard PvE co-op. Teammates can revive downed allies within a short window before the player fully respawns at a spawn point. A successful revive denies the opposing team a kill score. Experienced teams prioritize reviving downed members when opponents are not immediately pressuring the revive location.

Spawn points are assigned per team and rotate to prevent spawn camping. The engine enforces a minimum distance between a respawning player and active opponents, though this does not guarantee a safe spawn in small arenas under heavy contention.

## Tactics

Cross-fire positioning -- where two teammates occupy separate angles on a common enemy -- dramatically increases the difficulty of any single player defending against both simultaneously. This is the most transferable tactical concept from conventional FPS team deathmatch and applies directly to Conclave arenas.

Communication is limited to Warframe's in-game ping system during a match. Pre-match voice coordination through external tools is not prohibited but is rarely available for pickup groups. Most successful Team Annihilation play emerges from consistent pairs or small groups who develop shared habits around prioritizing flanks versus frontal defense.

Warframe selection in team context rewards roles. Frames with reliable mobility -- slide-attack speed, sprint multipliers, or parkour reach under Conclave scaling -- can pursue flanks effectively. Frames with shorter casting windows on defensive abilities can protect a teammate momentarily while the rest of the team collapses on a target.

## Weapon balance

Conclave weapon balance in Team Annihilation follows the same ruleset as Annihilation. Long-range precision weapons become more viable in team play because teammates create enough distraction to allow a sniper-style player to hold angles consistently. Coordinating a player on area-denial alongside teammates pushing into close quarters is a recognized tactic.

Explosive weapons retain their reduced blast radii from Conclave scaling. In tight corridors where team clusters occur, even reduced-radius explosives remain dangerous. The rule of thumb for new team players is to avoid weapons that threaten teammates in doorways and chokepoints.

## Rewards

Team Annihilation awards Conclave standing per match under the same formula as Annihilation: participation standing plus win bonuses. Team wins receive higher bonus multipliers than individual top-place finishes in free-for-all modes, incentivizing players who prefer cooperative play to grind standing efficiently in Team Annihilation.

The Teshin Conclave Syndicate standing earned in this mode unlocks Conclave-exclusive augments, cosmetics, and sigils. Several augments are sought by PvE-focused players for their effect in standard missions.

## Lore

Teshin frames Team Annihilation as a reflection of Old War squad doctrine. During the Old War, Warframes operated in coordinated strike teams rather than as solo operatives. He designed team modes to reintroduce that collaborative combat memory, believing that Tenno who train alone develop habits that leave gaps in real engagements where coordination is survival.

The Dax martial tradition from which Teshin comes emphasized unit cohesion over individual heroism. He considers Team Annihilation the closer analog to actual Tenno-era combat doctrine, and considers Annihilation's free-for-all format a necessary concession to those not yet ready for interdependence.
