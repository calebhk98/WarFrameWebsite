## Overview

Nidus is a parasitic warframe whose strength grows exponentially through combat via his signature Mutation stack mechanic. Unlike traditional warframes that rely on a static stat pool, Nidus accumulates Mutation stacks during missions, each stack increasing his Ability Strength and unlocking new mechanical layers in his abilities. His kit revolves around the Infestation, a living damage type that scales with ability power, crowd control, and self-sustaining gameplay loops. Nidus excels in prolonged endless missions (Survival, Defense, Infested Salvage) where extended engagement allows Mutation to spiral out of control, turning the frame into an unkillable plague of biological destruction.

With 455 health and 350 armor but zero shields, Nidus prioritizes tanking damage through armor scaling and his powerful passive regeneration mechanic. His energy pool of 100 pairs with zero passive energy regeneration, requiring careful management in the early game or reliance on energy orbs and team support. The frame's 1.0x sprint speed is baseline, offering no mobility bonus, but this is irrelevant given Nidus's crowdcontrol-focused playstyle that discourages repositioning. Nidus was introduced in Update 19.5 (December 2016) and remains one of the most unique and satisfying frames to pilot for players who enjoy mechanical depth and scaling-dependent gameplay.

## Abilities

### Virulence

Virulence erupts the ground beneath Nidus, creating a damaging fungal wall that travels outward. Each enemy struck loses energy (which Nidus gains) and takes Infestation damage. The critical mechanic: for every five enemies hit in a single cast, Nidus gains one Mutation stack. This is the primary tool for Mutation generation during high-enemy-count phases and scales exceptionally with ability strength and crowd-control augments. Virulence also benefits from Mutation stacks--at higher stack counts, the ability's damage output increases, creating a positive feedback loop. The energy steal is crucial for sustaining ability spam in early-game builds lacking efficiency mods.

### Larva

Larva spawns an infested pod that bursts with parasitic tendrils, pulling nearby enemies toward the center and holding them suspended. Enemies killed while held generate one Mutation stack per kill. In Update 29.5, this mechanic was added to enable aggressive Mutation farming strategies. Larva pairs exceptionally well with Ravenous--cast Larva to consolidate enemies, then cast Ravenous to allow maggots to feast on the held group, generating stacks from each kill. Larva is both a crowd-control tool and a Mutation generator, making it essential for Mutation scaling in team play and high-level content.

### Parasitic Link

Parasitic Link is a zero-cost ability that binds Nidus to an ally or enemy. Cast on allies, it grants both Nidus and the ally increased Ability Strength--stacking multiplicatively with other strength mods. This makes Nidus an exceptional support frame in squad play, buffing a Trinity's healing, a Saryn's damage, or a Harrow's survivability. Cast on enemies, the target takes a portion of damage that Nidus receives, allowing Nidus to trigger Mutation stacks through damage redirection and creating unique gameplay opportunities (e.g., using melee weapons while linked to an enemy to farm Mutation passively). The ability has 0 energy cost and no cooldown, enabling it to be recast or maintained indefinitely.

### Ravenous

Ravenous summons maggots that swarm nearby enemies and feed on them until the enemies are struck by Virulence or defeated. The maggots inherit all of Nidus's stat scaling including Ability Strength and Duration. Each enemy the maggots feast on generates one Mutation stack, and with higher Mutation counts, the maggots gain increased health and damage. Ravenous creates a biological multiplier effect--more Mutation stacks mean stronger maggots, which kill more enemies, generating more stacks. In coordinated squads or survival missions lasting 40+ minutes, Ravenous stacks can reach 50+, making Nidus nearly invincible. The ability's damage and crowd control both scale with Mutation, making it the frame's ultimate expression of exponential power growth.

## Passive

Nidus's passive grants invulnerability and full health restoration if he is killed while holding at least 5 Mutation stacks. This transforms Nidus's survivability from a defensive "don't die" check into a "die strategically" mechanic. In endgame content where sustained damage eventually overwhelms any defense, the passive enables Nidus to effectively trade health for Mutation resets. Many high-level Nidus players deliberately let themselves take lethal damage to consume stacks and regain full health, maintaining unkillable status. The passive requires maintaining Mutation generation to remain effective, making it a gameplay loop enabler rather than a passive safety net.

## Acquisition

Nidus's blueprint is obtained by completing the Glast Gambit quest, available to players who have finished The War Within. The quest rewards the Nidus blueprint directly upon completion. Component blueprints (Neuroptics, Chassis, Systems) drop from Infested Salvage missions on Eris/Oestrus exclusively in Rotation C, requiring extended mission clears (25+ minutes) to reach the drop table. Infested Salvage features a resource-gathering loop where players collect biomass to defend the objective, making farming inherently time-intensive compared to traditional assassination missions. The full frame takes 72 hours to build once all blueprints and materials are gathered. New players typically farm Nidus around Mastery Rank 15+ after unlocking The Glast Gambit, making him one of the late-early-game acquisitions rather than a starter choice.

## Playstyle

Nidus thrives in prolonged endless missions where enemy density remains high and time allows Mutation stacks to spiral into the stratosphere. The optimal playstyle is binary: early game (waves 0-3 in Defense, first 5 minutes of Survival), focus on Mutation generation through Larva and Virulence spam, maintaining energy via enemy energy drops and team Trinity support. Once Mutation reaches 20+ stacks, Ravenous becomes capable of holding entire rooms as maggots feast autonomously, enabling semi-AFK survival in high-level content. Parasitic Link should be cast on high-damage teammates to multiplicatively boost their output, or on tanky enemies to redirect damage and farm Mutation passively. The playstyle discourages aggression and instead rewards patience--Nidus is strongest when hanging back, casting Ravenous, and letting the Infestation work.

In team-based missions (Defense, Excavation), Nidus functions as both a crowd controller and a support frame via Parasitic Link. In solo play, Ravenous becomes the entire wincon; maggots scale so aggressively that they handle all damage and crowd control, leaving Nidus to cast Parasitic Link on self-buffed melee weapons or simply observe the biological cascade. Survival is Nidus's native environment--the mission type's emphasis on staying alive and resource rotation creates an ideal condition for Mutation scaling. The frame is comparatively weak in missions with strict time limits (void fissures, mobile defense) where Mutation cannot mature, making Nidus less universal than frames like Volt or Mesa.

## Lore

Nidus emerged from the Orokin's final desperate attempts to weaponize the Infestation itself. Where most warframes were engineered from Void-touched biomass, Nidus was constructed as a living symbiosis--Orokin human consciousness grafted into a sentient Infested colony. This hybrid nature explains his grotesque appearance and his ability to communicate with the Infestation at a biological level. The Infestation does not attack Nidus; instead, it responds to his will as a hive-organism extension of his own body. In the twisted logic of Orokin engineering, Nidus is not a warrior piloting the Infestation--he is the Infestation, wearing a humanoid form as a disguise.

The Grineer obtained Nidus's genetic template after the fall of the Orokin and weaponized it in their own bio-warfare programs, creating the Infestation as it persists in-game. Nidus Prime, released in Update 30.7, represents an earlier Orokin iteration with refined armor plating and more distinctive biological integration, suggesting the Orokin were still perfecting the symbiosis when Nidus was vaulted as a completed weapon. Lore-wise, each Mutation stack represents Nidus shedding his disguise and embracing his true nature as a Plague made flesh. At maximum Mutation, Nidus is effectively post-human--a pure biological cascade indistinguishable from the Infestation itself.
